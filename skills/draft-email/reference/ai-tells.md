# AI tells to avoid — all draft skills

Read this before writing the body of any draft. It lists wording that makes an email or note
sound machine-written. Patterns adapted from [blader/humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer)
(MIT).

## The template comes first

**Every fixed line in the skill's own template stays word for word.** That covers the salutation
(`Dear <Name>,`), set opening and closing lines (for example `Do let me know if you have any
further questions.`, `If you need further help or have any questions, just let us know. We're
here to assist you.`, `Please let us know if there's anything we can assist you with...`),
`Thank you.`, and the sign-off (`Regards,` / `Best Regards,` + name). They are house style, not
AI tells — never reword, shorten or drop them because of this list.

This list applies only to the text you write yourself: the explanation, the findings, the steps,
the progress line.

## Avoid in the body

- **Chatbot openers and filler.** "I hope this email finds you well", "Great question",
  "I'd be happy to help", "Certainly!". Start with the content.
- **Inflated words.** "delve", "crucial", "pivotal", "seamless", "robust", "leverage",
  "comprehensive", "ensure smooth operations". Say the plain thing: "use", "important", "works".
- **Inflated importance.** "This is a critical step in ensuring..." — state what the step does.
- **Sales tone.** "We are pleased to inform you", "rest assured", "we have gone the extra mile".
- **Stacked qualifiers.** "may potentially", "it seems that it might possibly". One hedge at
  most, and only when you really are unsure.
- **Forced groups of three.** "quickly, efficiently and accurately". List only what is true.
- **"Not X, but Y" framing** and arguing with an objection nobody raised.
- **Decorative bold or headings** in the body. Plain labels (`Steps:`, `Please note:`) carry
  the structure.
- **Long dashes as the default joiner.** Use a full stop, a comma, or ` - ` as the house examples
  do.
- **Curly quotes** (“ ” ‘ ’). Use straight quotes.
- **Re-explaining what the reader already knows** — their own steps, their own error, the
  background they gave you.
- **A summing-up line or moral at the end of the body** ("In summary, ...", "This should resolve
  everything."). The template's own closing lines already end the email.

## Check before handing back

Read the body once against this list and fix what you find. Do not run a separate rewrite pass
over the whole draft, and do not touch the template lines.
